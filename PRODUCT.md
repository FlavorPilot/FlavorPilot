# Product Model

## Product promise

TasteCraft helps a cook understand what is happening inside a specific dish—not merely which two ingredients are commonly paired.

A user enters ingredients, quantity, and preparation. The application models the current composition, explains its main strengths and weaknesses, and proposes practical next moves.

## Core distinction

### Compatibility

How naturally a candidate ingredient works with the ingredients already present. It combines explicit pair knowledge, aroma overlap, useful contrasts, texture, and intensity.

### Utility

How useful that candidate is **right now**. Utility also considers the current sensory deficit, selected direction, simulated balance change, and texture improvement.

A fatty ingredient can be compatible with salmon but have low utility in an already fatty composition.

## Primary workflow

1. Create or name a dish.
2. Add an ingredient.
3. Set grams and preparation.
4. Observe live score and profile changes.
5. Read the main issue and best next move.
6. Add a recommendation or edit quantities.
7. Save as public, unlisted, or private.
8. Publish, discover, and remix while preserving authorship lineage.

## Visibility

- **Public:** discoverable and visible in the author profile.
- **Unlisted:** accessible only through a share token/link.
- **Private:** visible only to the owner.

The MVP pricing hypothesis gives free users three private dishes and unlimited public creation. A paid plan unlocks unlimited private work and version history. This must be presented as a positive private-workspace benefit, never as silent forced publication.

## Current MVP persona hypothesis

The first user is an individual cook, culinary student, active home enthusiast, or chef who develops dishes independently and can subscribe without an enterprise sales process.

Restaurants and kitchen teams are a later expansion after individual product-market fit.

## Monetization hypothesis

### Free

- Core constructor and live score.
- Public discovery and remix.
- Limited private drafts.
- Basic deterministic explanation.

### Pro Chef

- Unlimited private dishes and drafts.
- Full “add/remove/replace/how much/why” workflow.
- Version history and collections.
- Expanded analyses and controlled AI explanation allowance.

### Chef Studio

- Costing, yield, allergens, nutrition, scaling, technical cards, export, and purchasing support.

### Kitchen / Enterprise

- Roles, approvals, shared standards, menu economics, API access, and integrations.

Prices shown in the UI are product hypotheses and should be validated through real payment behavior.

## Social differentiation

The social layer is not only a feed of recipes. A remix preserves parentage and can show exactly which ingredient, preparation, or quantity changed and how the modeled profile changed. Long term, this can become an evolution tree for dishes.

## Trust principles

- Scores must be explainable.
- Confidence must be visible when data quality differs.
- Recommendations must never be sponsored secretly.
- Users must understand who can see a dish before saving.
- Professional recipes may be commercially sensitive.
- “Showcase” visibility may eventually reveal a concept while hiding exact grams, process, temperatures, costing, and notes.
